When you were born, no one expected you to run. Growth was a process. Your muscles had to develop. Your balance had to be strengthened. You were lifted, supported, and encouraged until one day, you had enough strength to move. Today, you walk without even thinking about it. That’s how spiritual growth works. Sometimes, as a young Christian, you may wonder, “If I’m truly God’s child, why don’t I always get the results I expect?” But your identity isn’t the issue. The Bible says in Romans 8:16 that the Spirit Himself bears witness with our spirit that we’re the children of God.

You’re God’s child—settled and established in Him. The question isn’t about your identity; it’s about your training. Spiritual maturity requires practice. You must be trained in righteousness and exercised in the Word. Christianity isn’t a passive experience; it’s an active life. We’re Word practitioners. You practise love when it’s difficult. You practise faith when circumstances challenge you. You practise joy when pressure mounts. You practise your divine authority until it becomes your natural response. James 1:22 reminds us to be doers of the Word and not hearers only. That means putting the Word to work daily.

When the Word says you’re victorious, act like it. When it says you’re blessed, speak like it. When it says you have dominion, live like it. Keep training yourself. Keep practising who you are in Christ. Just as a child eventually walks with confidence and ease, you’ll grow to a point where victory, health, wisdom, and prosperity flow effortlessly from your life. Refuse to be discouraged by temporary stumbles. Stay with the Word. Praise God!

## Prayer

Dear Father, thank you for training me through your Word and by your Spirit in the way of righteousness. I walk in your truth and function from the Kingdom’s perspective. My faith is strengthened, my understanding deepened, and I live every day in mastery and victory because I live in and by your Word, in Jesus’ Name. Amen.

## Act

Identify one situation today where you’ll consciously respond with the Word instead of your feelings—choose love, faith, or joy on purpose.
