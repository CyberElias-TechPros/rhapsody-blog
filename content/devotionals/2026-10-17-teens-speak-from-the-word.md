Faith isn’t about making empty confessions or merely repeating words. For example, saying “I’m fine, I’m fine, I’m fine” when you’re stressed about school, exams, or friendships doesn’t make things better. The scripture above says, “I believed, and therefore I spoke,” and in Romans 10:10, believing comes first, then speaking. What’s the basis of your “I’m fine”? Faith rests on what Christ has already done.

So, when you say, “I refuse to be sick,” it’s because God has already given you divine health. Even if you feel tired, anxious, or stressed, when someone asks, “How are you?” you can say, “I’m excellent,” because God’s life in you is stronger than sickness, fear, or weakness. The same goes for declaring, “I refuse to be poor,” because Christ has made you rich. 2 Corinthians 8:9 says, “For you know the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ, that though He was rich, yet for your sakes He became poor, that you through His poverty might become rich.” Being “rich in Christ” isn’t just about money; it’s about blessings, favour, and opportunities.

Our faith in who we are in Christ is the foundation of our confessions. It brings God’s Word into manifestation. We can also speak new realities into existence by God’s Word (Greek: “rhema”). Whether it’s courage for a test, peace in friendships, or creativity for a project, speaking God’s Word activates change. Affirming your health, success, and victory is acknowledging what Christ already made yours. Glory to God!

## Prayer

Dear Father, thank you for granting me an understanding of the true basis of faith. My words are full of power because they’re anchored in the finished works of Christ. As I declare your Word, the realities of my inheritance in Christ, my health, wealth, and victory are activated and manifested, in Jesus’ Name. Amen.

## Act

Take a scripture on any area of concern you have, meditate on it and declare it out loud.
