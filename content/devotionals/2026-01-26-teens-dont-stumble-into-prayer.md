A certain sculptor sat before a massive block of marble. He didn’t rush to strike it with his chisel. Instead, he spent hours sketching, envisioning, and aligning his mind with the form he intended to reveal. Only then did he begin to work.

Prayer is much like that. Many approach God’s throne hastily, with unformed words and scattered thoughts, hoping that sheer length or repetition will suffice. But the Lord Jesus taught us in Matthew 6:7 not to pray “vain repetitions, as the heathen do: for they think that they shall be heard for their much speaking.” True prayer is not careless utterance; it is a spiritual alignment, a purposeful engagement with the Father.

When you plan your prayer, you bring order to your fellowship. You’re not reducing the time spent with God— you’re elevating it. By taking a moment to write down the matters of your heart—your desires, the areas where you need His guidance, and even the thoughts He has already impressed upon you—you position yourself to pray with clarity and faith.

Then, as you yield to the Spirit in tongues, you transcend human vocabulary. You speak mysteries, articulating divine secrets that only heaven can decode. It’ll help to always take a moment to write down the things you want to talk to the Lord about. Think about the areas of your life where you desire His guidance, intervention and blessings. Also, consider the thoughts and insights He might have already stirred in your heart. Then, you speak in tongues. Through speaking in tongues, you articulate divine mysteries and gain insight into realities beyond human comprehension.

## Prayer

Dear Heavenly Father, I thank you for the privilege of prayer and for the wisdom of your Word. I pray with reverence and purpose, guided by your Spirit to speak words that are filled with faith and divine insight, making my words in prayer effective. Thank you for the assurance of answers, for you hear me always, in Jesus’ Name. Amen.

## Act

From today, begin writing out what you wish to pray about on a piece of paper or a journal before proceeding to pray at your personal times of prayer.
